# Research with evidence

Use Federated Research for broad web and scientific discovery, Context7 for current library documentation, and the built-in `webfetch` tool to inspect decisive primary sources directly.

1. List the exact unknowns and the decision each answer controls.
2. Search broadly enough to find competing explanations and primary sources. Treat search summaries as leads, not evidence.
3. Deduplicate equivalent results and prefer specifications, official documentation, repositories, release notes, datasets, and original research.
4. Fetch every decisive primary-source URL with `webfetch`. Record its title, canonical URL, publisher or author, publication or revision date when available, and the claim it supports.
5. Preserve citation metadata and any generated tables, excerpts, or reports as artifacts. Never put credentials in citations or artifacts.
6. Cross-check material claims, state uncertainty and conflicts explicitly, and verify that the final answer satisfies each research question before reporting completion.

Separate verified facts, observations, inferences, and unresolved assumptions. A successful search or fetch call is not by itself proof that the conclusion is correct.
